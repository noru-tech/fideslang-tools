# W002: uncategorized dataset field

**Severity:** warning · **Checked by:** [`src/validate/rules/hygiene.rs`](../../src/validate/rules/hygiene.rs)

**Rule:** every leaf field of a dataset should have at least one data category.

## What it checks

`fl` walks each dataset's collections and fields, including nested `fields`, and warns about every
field without sub-fields whose `data_categories` is missing, `null` or empty. A field that only groups
sub-fields is not checked itself; its leaves are.

## Why it matters

In a Fides dataset the data categories on a field say what kind of data it holds. That is how a data
map answers "where do we keep email addresses?", and Fides privacy request policies select the fields
to access or erase by data category, so a field without one is invisible to them. Fields that hold no
personal data should still say so, for example with a `system.*` category.

## Controls

If you derive the categories of personal data in a record of processing activities (GDPR
Art. 30(1)(c)) or locate data for access and erasure requests (Art. 15, Art. 17) from your datasets,
an uncategorized field is data the record and the request do not cover. `fl` reports the missing
label; it cannot tell whether the field holds personal data.

## How to fix

Add the most specific category that fits, for example `user.contact.email` rather than `user`.
`fl taxonomy search WORD` and `fl taxonomy tree categories --descriptions` help you choose.
`fl stats PATH` lists every uncategorized field and the categorized-field coverage, which is a
convenient way to work through a large dataset.

## Suppress or promote

`--deny W002` (or `-W`) makes it fail the run. `--allow W002` drops it, for example while you
categorize a large legacy dataset; `fl stats` still reports the coverage.
